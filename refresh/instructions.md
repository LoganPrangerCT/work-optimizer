Priority calibration (calculate runway using snapshot serverTime and the actual due date; examples are relative, not fixed dates):
- P5 Critical: normally a consequential deadline is within roughly 1–2 days and the remaining work, coordination, or risk means it needs attention immediately. - P4 High: substantial or important work needs planned progress now, typically with a few days left, but is not yet an immediate crisis. Calibration: a roughly three-hour Final Project Proposal due in four days is P4. Increase urgency as the deadline approaches and the remaining work warrants it; a major project with genuinely long lead time can need P4 earlier.
- P3 Standard: work needs attention in the near term but has reasonable runway relative to its remaining effort. This is not a default for every required or graded task.
- P2 Low: required or important work with ample runway can wait comfortably, unless evidence shows an earlier coordination deadline or substantial lead time. Importance may make a distant item P2 rather than P1, but importance alone does not make it urgent.
- P1 Minor or distant: work can safely wait a long time relative to its effort and dependencies, even if eventually required. Calibration: an internship form requiring supervisor input due December 10, when about five weeks remain, is P1 or P2—not P3 merely because it is needed for course grades. Unknown effort or the fact that someone else participates is not proof of an immediate coordination deadline. Raise it when there is evidence of a real near-term dependency, unusually long lead time, or shrinking runway.
